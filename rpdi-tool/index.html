Capped at 50 — Skin's share of total weight flattens out well before this, so more claims add noise, not signal.

The Formula

        
D = 1 − (Σ weight × accuracy) / (Σ weight)
L = (wS·S + wP·P + wF·F + wE·E) / (wS+wP+wF+wE)
RPDI = 0.6(D) + 0.4(L)
Gap = D − L  (signed — see Classification Scales below)

Spine-Floor Rule

        

          If the single tagged Absolute Premise claim scores ≤0.3, the final classification cannot
          register below High — regardless of the raw weighted average. Raw D is still calculated
          and shown; only the label overrides. Deliberately scoped to the Absolute Premise claim
          alone, not any Spine claim generally — an ordinary Spine claim taking real dramatic
          license shouldn't automatically floor a film; only fabricating the single plot point the
          entire story depends on does. Other Spine claims still carry full weight-5 influence on
          the raw D score itself, just without this automatic override.
        

      


      

        
Tier Assignment — The Decision Procedure

        

          Applied in strict order for every claim. Step 1 — the Spine Test, checked first, always:
          "If this claim were false, does the film's central premise structurally stop holding together —
          not just become less compelling, but stop making sense as the story it claims to be?" If yes,
          it's Spine regardless of surface content (the existing 2–3 claim cap still governs how many
          claims can actually hold this tier). If no, classify by content type: Soul asks why a character
          acted; Skeleton asks what documented event occurred; Flesh asks whether it's a personal or
          relationship detail that isn't load-bearing or broadly historical; Skin asks whether it's purely
          visual with no bearing on plot or motive. If a properly specific claim still fits nowhere, the
          claim is too vague — tighten it rather than discard it. A claim mixing two content types
          (an event and a motive) gets split into two separate claims, never forced into one tier.
        

      


      

        
First-Encounter Rule

        

          Claims are logged in the order they're narratively encountered, decided on significance and
          specificity alone — never on a prediction about how they'll score. Once logged, a claim cannot
          be swapped for a different one in the same tier just because it might score better or worse.
          The only valid reason to remove a logged claim is a structural defect — too vague, not a real
          checkable assertion — never preference for a different candidate.
        

      


      

        
Generalization Validity Rule

        

          A claim about one specific individual's action can never be reworded into a claim about their
          nationality or group — that's the researcher's inference, not something the film asserts. A
          group-level claim only becomes extractable when the film shows the same pattern through
          multiple distinct instances, not one scene — and even then it's capped at 0.3, the same logic
          as the mythology-only ceiling: inferring something categorical from a handful of instances
          remains weak evidence.

Gap = D − L (signed)Classification

          
≤ −0.203Extreme Gaslighting

          
−0.203 to −0.05Gaslighting

          
−0.05 to +0.05Honest / Consistent

          
+0.05 to +0.25Sanitized Fabrication

          
≥ +0.25Extreme Sanitized Fabrication

        

        

          Gap is signed, not an absolute value — direction carries real meaning and is
          never overridden by statistics. Negative means portrayal outpaces distortion:
          accurate facts wrapped in manipulative dramatization (Gaslighting). Positive
          means distortion outpaces portrayal: fabricated content presented with false
          calm (Sanitized Fabrication). The Honest/Consistent deadband (±0.05) matches
          the accuracy scale's own finest granularity — anything smaller is measurement
          noise, not a real signal. The Gaslighting/Extreme split (−0.203) is the real
          median of this project's 14 negative-Gap films as of this calibration —
          data-driven, not a round number. The Sanitized Fabrication side still uses a
          fixed, principled threshold (0.25): only one positive-Gap film has ever been
          scored, nowhere near enough to calibrate a real statistical boundary yet.
        

      


      

        
Feel Score Weighting Rule

        

          All four dimensions (S/P/F/E) default to weight 1. A weight may only be raised with specific,
          citable evidence from the film itself — never genre assumption alone.
        

      


      

        
Known Limitations

        

          Single-rater subjectivity (no inter-rater check exists). Ceilings and the 0.6/0.4 RPDI
          weighting are judgment calls, not derived constants. Gap thresholds are provisional.
          Skin's actual share drifts slightly above 25% at higher claim counts since it absorbs
          rounding. No film has yet been fully re-verified under this current rule set.
